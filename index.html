01

About

Right now, I lead the AI Community of Practice at Avanade, designing and delivering AI-driven solutions for clients in Financial Services, Government, Defence, Utilities, Mining, and CMT. My work sits between business need and technology capability. I translate strategic vision into proofs of concept and deployable systems. I mentor junior team members, with a focus on building women into leadership in technology.

For twenty-five years, I've grounded my work in design thinking and human-centred approaches to complex problems. I hold a research master's in design innovation because I learned early that the technology is usually the easy part. The hard part is understanding what matters, designing for real use, and building teams and capability that sustains the work. That thread runs through every role I've held, from risk and process design through to AI strategy.

If you've been in a room where someone asked the question everyone hoped had gone away, it was probably me. I'm the one who'll push back on "that's how we've always done it" and won't let a dodgy assumption slide. Some call it annoying. I call it caring about getting it right. In twenty-five years, that's proven surprisingly useful.

06

Off the clock

Black cockatoos

The red-tailed black cockatoo is possibly the most elegant thing in the Australian sky. It's a kind of aggressive grace. I've spent mornings watching them, usually because I should be doing something else. There's something about their single-mindedness that feels like home.

Chillis

I grow hot chillis the way some people grow roses, with devoted obsession. Every summer I'm experimenting with heat, flavour, and which varieties will outlast the local pests. It's strategic gardening. It's also just deeply satisfying to bite into something you grew yourself.

The garden

A working garden teaches you patience and brutality in equal measure. Some things don't make it. Some years you get aphids instead of eggplants. But there's a kind of honest productivity in it that I find genuinely restorative. It's the opposite of management meetings.

Formula One

I've followed F1 since I could sit still long enough to watch a race. It's not about the glamour. It's the strategic complexity that gets me. Team orders, tyre management, reading the track, incremental gains, and the engineering brilliance required to win by half a second. It's strategy and execution in its purest form. I respect that deeply.
