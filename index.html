About

Over 20 years, I've worked with teams and organisations across various sectors—you'll know the places. I've led through change, built capability, and delivered programmes that actually moved the needle. I'm most useful when things are complicated and a straight answer matters more than a comfortable one.

I approach problems the way a gardener approaches a plot: you need to understand the soil, the light, the pests—not just plant what looks good in the catalogue. Strategy without craft falls apart. Delivery without strategy is just busy work. The best work lives at their intersection.

If you've been in a room where someone—usually me—asked the question everyone hoped had gone away, I'm probably that person. I'm the one who'll push back on "that's how we've always done it" and won't let a dodgy assumption slide. Some call it annoying. I call it caring about getting it right. In a 20-year career, that's proven surprisingly useful.

Off the clock

Black cockatoos

The red-tailed black cockatoo is possibly the most elegant thing in the Australian sky—a kind of aggressive grace. I've spent mornings watching them, usually because I should be doing something else. There's something about their single-mindedness that feels like home.

Chillis

I grow hot chillis the way some people grow roses—with devotion bordering on obsession. Every summer I'm experimenting with heat, flavour, and which varieties will outlast the local pests. It's strategic gardening. It's also just deeply satisfying to bite into something you grew yourself.

The garden

A working garden teaches you patience and brutality in equal measure. Some things don't make it. Some years you get aphids instead of aubergines. But there's a kind of honest productivity in it that I find genuinely restorative. It's the opposite of management meetings.

Formula One

I've followed F1 since I could sit still long enough to watch a race. It's not about the glamour—it's the strategic complexity. Team orders, tyre management, reading the track, incremental gains, and the engineering brilliance required to win by half a second. It's strategy and execution in its purest form. I respect that deeply.
